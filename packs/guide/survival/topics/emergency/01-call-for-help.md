# Call or text 911

**Call 911 even with one bar.** A 911 call can go through on any carrier's tower that reaches you, not only your own.

**If the call fails, text 911.** Texting does not work everywhere; an automatic reply saying it failed means try again another way. Put your location and the help you need in the first text. Use plain words, no abbreviations.

**Say where you are first.** Your phone's map or compass app can often show latitude and longitude without signal. Then say:

- what happened and who is hurt, and how
- how many people are with you
- landmarks, clothing colors, and what gear you have
- your phone number

**Stay on the line** and do what the call taker says. Do not hang up until they tell you to.

**Keep the phone warm and charged.** Cold drains batteries fast, so carry it inside your clothes, close to your body.

# No signal?

**Satellite SOS.** Some phones and satellite messengers can send an SOS by satellite. Get a clear view of the sky and follow the prompts.

**Personal locator beacon (PLB)**, for a real emergency only:

1. Move to open sky, away from cliffs and thick trees.
2. Raise the antenna as the label shows and keep it pointing up.
3. Turn it on and **leave it on** until you are rescued or rescuers tell you to stop. The battery lasts at least 24 hours.

Most cannot confirm that help is coming: stay put and keep signaling.

**Higher, open ground** often gets signal where a valley does not. Go only if it is close and safe.

**Save the battery.** Dim the screen. Between tries, use airplane mode or turn the phone off; check it at set times. If 911 already has you, follow their advice.

**Send your coordinates** in every message.

NOTE: Register a PLB with NOAA before your trip. Set one off by mistake? Call the U.S. Air Force rescue center, 1-800-851-3051.
