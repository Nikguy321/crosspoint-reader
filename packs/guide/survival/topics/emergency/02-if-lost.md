# STOP

The moment you think you are lost, **STOP**. Panic, not the woods, is the real danger: it makes people rush, wander and get hurt.

1. **Stop.** Stand still. Sit down, take slow breaths, drink some water and eat a snack. Do not walk on "just to see".
2. **Think.** Where did you last know where you were? What do you have with you? Who knows your plan, and when will they expect you back?
3. **Observe.** Look around: landmarks, the weather, how much daylight is left. Check your map, compass or GPS.
4. **Plan.** Decide what to do next, calmly. Go back only if you are sure of the way and it is safe. If you are not sure, stay where you are.

Give yourself time. Big choices made in the first shock are often bad ones. Wait until you feel steady, then decide.

+++

# Stay put

**Searchers find people who stay in one place.** They start from where you were last known to be. Every mile you wander widens the area they must cover. Help can take hours or days.

- Camp at the edge of an open area: seen from the air, but out of the wind and rain. Avoid dead trees and dry streambeds.
- Mark your spot. Spread bright clothing or gear in the open.
- Blow a whistle three times, rest, repeat. It carries farther than shouting.
- Get warm and dry before dark. Shelter and water come before food.

**Move only if your spot is unsafe**: rising water, falling rock, fire, or avalanche ground. Go a short way to safety, then stop again.

If you must leave for good, leave a note or arrow at the spot: when you left, which way, and how you are.

NOTE: Children: pick a tree, hug it, and stay there. Answer every whistle or shout. Never hide from searchers.
