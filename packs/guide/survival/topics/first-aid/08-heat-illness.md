# Heat exhaustion

**Signs:**

- heavy sweating
- cool, pale, clammy skin
- fast, weak pulse
- nausea or vomiting, muscle cramps
- tiredness, weakness, dizziness, headache
- fainting

**What to do:**

1. Stop all activity. Move to shade or a cool place.
2. Loosen clothing and take off extra layers.
3. Put cool, wet cloths on the body, or wash the head, face and neck with cold water. Fan the person.
4. Sip water or a sports drink.
5. Rest until fully recovered; do not push on.

**Get medical help** if they are throwing up, get worse, or still have symptoms after 1 hour.

NOTE: Drink often in the heat, but no more than about 1.5 quarts (1.5 L) an hour. Too much plain water can also make a person sick and confused.

WARNING: Confusion or odd behavior means heat stroke. Go to the next page now.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Heat stroke is an emergency

**Signs:** confusion, slurred speech or strange behavior; hot skin that may be dry or sweaty; very high body temperature, 103 F (39.4 C) or more; seizures; passing out.

1. **Call 911 or send an SOS.** Heat stroke can kill.
2. **Cool first, fast.** Do not wait for help.
3. **Best:** put the person in cold water up to the neck, in a calm, shallow spot of a stream or lake. Hold them so their head stays above water. Take them out after about 15 minutes, or sooner once they think clearly.
4. **Otherwise:** in shade, take off outer clothing, soak the skin with water and fan hard. Put cold packs or cold wet cloths on the neck, armpits and groin.
5. With this method, keep cooling until they think clearly again.
6. Give nothing to drink while they are confused.
7. If they stop responding but still breathe, use the recovery position. Watch their breathing.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
