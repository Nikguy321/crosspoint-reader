# Diarrhea and vomiting

The danger is losing water and salt. **Sip small amounts often.** After vomiting, wait a few minutes, then start small sips again.

- **Best:** a packet of oral rehydration salts mixed into treated water, usually 1 quart (1 L). Adults need about 3 quarts (3 L) a day while sick.
- **No packet?** Keep sipping treated water or other clean drinks. Carry packets in your first-aid kit.
- **Mild cases:** any clean drink helps. Large amounts of very sweet drinks can make diarrhea worse.
- Rest. Eat plain food when you feel hungry.

**Get medical help** if there is:

- blood in the stool
- a high fever
- vomiting that will not let fluids stay down
- signs of dehydration: very little dark urine, dizziness, confusion
- no better after a day or two, or getting worse

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Stay clean

Most camp stomach illness spreads from hands, water and waste.

- **Wash hands** with soap and water for 20 seconds before handling food, before eating, after the toilet, and after caring for wounds or a sick person.
- No soap and water? Use hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol. Wipe off dirt first; it works poorly on dirty hands.
- **Catholes:** dig 6 to 8 inches (15 to 20 cm) deep, at least 200 feet (60 m) from water, camp and trails. Cover and hide it after.
- Pack out used toilet paper in a sealed bag.
- Wash dishes and yourself 200 feet (60 m) from streams and lakes. Never put soap in the water.
- Treat all drinking water, and use treated water for brushing teeth and washing dishes.
- Keep a sick person away from the group's food and water.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Altitude illness

**Mild altitude sickness** is common above about 8,000 feet (2,400 m). It starts 2 to 12 hours after arriving high: headache, plus nausea, poor appetite, dizziness or tiredness.

1. **Do not go higher to sleep** while you have symptoms.
2. Rest at the same height and drink to thirst.
3. **Getting worse? Go down** at least 1,000 feet (300 m).

**Emergency, descend now, with help:**

- confusion, stumbling as if drunk, or very sleepy
- short of breath while resting, a wet cough, or pink or bloody spit

Send an SOS. Never leave the person alone or send them down alone.

**Prevent it:** above about 10,000 feet (3,000 m), sleep no more than 1,650 feet (500 m) higher each night, and add a rest night every 3,300 feet (1,000 m).

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
