# Snakebite: what to do

1. **Move well away from the snake.**
2. **Call 911 or send an SOS** right away. Do not wait for symptoms. A venomous bite may need antivenom at a hospital.
3. **Keep calm and still.** Sit or lie down.
4. **Take off rings, watches and tight clothing** near the bite before it swells.
5. Wash the bite with soap and water. Cover it with a clean, dry dressing.
6. **Keep the bitten arm or leg still**, in a comfortable position at about heart level. A loose splint helps.
7. **Mark the edge of the swelling** on the skin with a pen and write the time beside it. Mark it again as it spreads.
8. **Get to a hospital.** If you must walk out, go slowly and rest often.

A photo of the snake from a safe distance can help doctors, but it is not needed.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Snakebite: what NOT to do

Old advice still gets passed around. All of these can make things worse:

WARNING: Do NOT cut the bite or try to suck out the venom.

WARNING: Do NOT put a tight band or tourniquet on the limb.

WARNING: Do NOT put ice on the bite or soak it in water.

WARNING: Do NOT try to catch or kill the snake. Even a dead snake or a cut-off head can still bite.

WARNING: Do NOT use electric shock or folk remedies.

WARNING: Do NOT drink alcohol or take aspirin, ibuprofen or naproxen for the pain.

**In the Northwest,** rattlesnakes are the only dangerous venomous snakes. In Washington most live in dry country east of the Cascades. Other snakes may bite, so wash any bite well and watch it for infection.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
