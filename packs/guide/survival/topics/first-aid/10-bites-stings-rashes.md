# Bee and wasp stings

1. **Get the stinger out fast.** Scrape it off with a fingernail or card edge, or wipe it with gauze. Do not squeeze it or use tweezers.
2. Wash it with soap and water.
3. Ease swelling with a cold pack or ice wrapped in cloth.
4. Do not scratch it.
5. Stay with them; watch for an allergic reaction.

**Severe allergic reaction** can start within minutes:

- swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat
- hives spreading over the body
- wheezing, trouble breathing or swallowing
- dizziness, fainting, vomiting

**Use their epinephrine auto-injector** at once: press it into the outer thigh, even through clothing. **Call 911 or send an SOS** even if they get better. No better after 5 to 10 minutes and no help yet? Use a second injector if they have one.

NOTE: People with severe allergies should carry two injectors and show the group where they are.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Animal and spider bites

**Mammal bites and scratches.** Wash right away with lots of soap and water for several minutes. Control bleeding, cover it, and leave it open (see Wounds). **See a doctor soon.** Rabies is deadly once symptoms start, but shots given in time prevent it.

**Bats.** Any contact with a bat needs a doctor, even with no mark. Bat bites can be too small to see. A bat found where someone was sleeping counts too.

Stay away from wild animals that act strangely or seem tame.

**Spider bites.**

1. Wash the bite with soap and water.
2. Put on a cloth soaked in cold water, or ice wrapped in cloth.
3. Raise the bitten part if you can.
4. Get medical care.

**Black widow** bites can hurt at first, then the pain spreads to the chest, belly or whole body, with cramps, sweating and nausea. Get medical care quickly.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Poison oak and poison ivy

**Leaves of three, let it be.** Poison oak and poison ivy have three leaflets on each leaf stem. Poison oak is usually a shrub with leaves shaped a bit like oak leaves. Poison ivy is often a hairy, ropelike vine or a low plant.

Their oil causes an itchy, blistering rash. It sticks to clothes, tools and pets.

**After touching it:**

1. **Rinse the skin right away** with soap, such as dish soap, and lots of cool water. Rubbing alcohol works too.
2. Wash exposed clothes separately in hot water with detergent.
3. Clean tools and gear with soap and water or rubbing alcohol.
4. Try not to scratch.

WARNING: Never burn these plants. The smoke can cause severe lung reactions.

**Call 911** if there is swelling of the face or throat, or trouble breathing.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
