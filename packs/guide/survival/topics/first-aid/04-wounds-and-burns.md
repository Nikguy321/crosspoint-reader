# Clean a wound

1. Stop any bleeding first.
2. Wash your hands. Wear gloves if you have them. Try not to touch the wound with your fingers.
3. Take off rings, watches and tight clothing near the wound before it swells.
4. **Flush the wound with lots of clean water:** treated, boiled and cooled, or bottled. Pour or squirt it in until it looks clean.
5. Wash around the wound with soap and clean water.
6. Pick out loose dirt and splinters with clean tweezers. Leave anything stuck deep in the wound; pad around it and get medical care.
7. Pat dry and cover with a clean dressing or cloth.

NOTE: Keep open wounds out of flood water, salt water and swamp water.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Do not close dirty wounds

**Leave dirty wounds, bites and punctures open** under a clean dressing. Closing them can trap germs inside and cause a bad infection.

- Pull the edges together with tape only on a small, clean, fresh cut.
- Look at the wound and change the dressing every day, or sooner if it gets wet or dirty.
- Keep it as clean and dry as you can.

Get medical care as soon as you can for:

- something stuck in the wound
- any animal bite
- a puncture from a dirty object
- a deep or gaping cut, or one that will not stop bleeding
- signs of infection (next page)

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

+++

# Infection signs

Watch every wound, even small ones. Get medical care if you see:

- redness or swelling that spreads, or red streaks moving away from it
- pus or oozing
- pain that gets worse instead of better
- fever or chills

**Get help urgently** if the person also becomes confused, short of breath, has a racing heart, extreme pain, or clammy, sweaty skin. These can be signs of sepsis, a life-threatening reaction to infection.

Meanwhile keep the wound clean, change the dressing, and keep the person warm and drinking.

**Tetanus.** Before a trip, check that your tetanus shot is up to date: a booster every 10 years. After a dirty wound or a puncture, ask a doctor whether you need one sooner.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Burns

1. **Stop the burning.** Put out flames, move away from the heat.
2. **Cool the burn** under cool running water for 10 to 20 minutes. Use cool water, not ice or icy water.
3. Take off rings, watches, belts and tight clothing near the burn before it swells. Leave any clothing that is stuck to the skin.
4. Cover loosely with a clean, dry cloth or nonstick gauze.
5. **Do not break blisters.** An open blister can get infected.
6. Keep the rest of the person warm. Cooling a large burn can chill them.

WARNING: Do NOT put butter, oil, egg, creams, lotions or home remedies on any burn.

A small, unblistered burn may get a thin layer of petroleum jelly or aloe.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

+++

# Serious burns

**Get medical care** for a burn that:

- is larger than 2 inches (5 cm) or has blisters
- is on the face, eyes, hands, feet, groin or a joint
- is very painful, or numb and painless

**Call 911 or send an SOS** if the burn:

- is large, or deep: dry and leathery, white, brown or black
- was caused by electricity or chemicals
- came with smoke: hoarse voice, coughing, soot around the nose or mouth, or trouble breathing, even if they seem fine.

While you wait:

1. Cool and cover the burn as on the last page.
2. **Electricity:** do not touch the person until the power is off.
3. **Chemicals:** brush off any powder and take off soaked clothing, then flush with lots of running water. Rinse eyes for 10 to 15 minutes.
4. Treat for shock: lay them flat, insulate them from the ground and keep them warm.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
