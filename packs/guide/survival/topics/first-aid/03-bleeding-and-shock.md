# Stop the bleeding

1. Make sure it is safe. Call 911 or send an SOS, or have someone do it.
2. Find where the blood is coming from. Cut or pull clothing away to see.
3. Put something between you and the blood: gloves, cloth, a plastic bag.
4. **Press hard, straight down on the wound** with cloth or gauze. Lean your weight on it. It may hurt them; keep pressing.
5. **Do not lift it to look.** If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top and keep pressing.
6. **Deep wound in an arm, leg, armpit or groin** you cannot press closed? Stuff cloth or gauze firmly down into it until it is full, then press hard on top. Never stuff a chest or belly wound; just press on it.
7. Keep pressing until help takes over. Swap with a helper if you tire.

An arm or leg that keeps bleeding heavily needs a tourniquet (page 3).

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Pressure bandage

![Pressure dressing tied over a wound](atp-p25-1)

Once bleeding slows, put a thick pad on the wound. Wrap a cloth strip firmly around it and tie the knot over the pad. Check beyond the wrap: fingers or toes must stay warm and pink. Numb, cold or blue? Rewrap it a little looser.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Tourniquet

For an arm or leg bleeding that hard pressure will not stop.

1. Keep pressing on the wound while someone gets the tourniquet.
2. Put it **2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm) above the wound**, between the wound and the heart. Never on a joint. Cannot see the wound? Put it as high on the limb as you can.
3. It can go over clothing. Pull the strap tight.
4. **Twist the rod until the bleeding stops.** It will hurt a lot; that is expected.
5. Lock the rod in place. Write the time on the tourniquet or the skin.
6. Still bleeding? Put a second one just above the first.
7. **Leave it on.** Only medical staff take it off.

No tourniquet? Use a cloth band at least 2 inches (5 cm) wide with a stick to twist it. Never use wire, cord or thin rope. A real tourniquet works better; carry one.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Shock

Shock means the body is not getting enough blood flow. Heavy bleeding is a common cause. Signs:

- pale or gray, cold, sweaty skin
- fast breathing and a fast, weak pulse
- weak, thirsty, anxious, confused or sleepy

What to do:

1. Call 911 or send an SOS. Shock needs a hospital fast.
2. Stop any bleeding and treat the cause.
3. Lay the person flat on their back.
4. Put insulation under them and cover them. Keep them warm, even on a mild day.
5. Give nothing to eat or drink.
6. Stay with them. Talk calmly and watch their breathing.
7. If they vomit or stop responding but still breathe, use the recovery position.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
