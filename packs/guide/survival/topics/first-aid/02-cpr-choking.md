# Hands-only CPR

Use it when an adult is **not responding and not breathing normally**, or only gasping.

1. Call 911 or send an SOS, or have someone else do it. Send someone for an AED if one is near.
2. Lay the person on their back on a firm surface.
3. Heel of one hand in the center of the chest, other hand on top, arms straight.
4. **Push hard and fast:** at least 2 inches (5 cm) deep, 100 to 120 times a minute.
5. Let the chest come all the way back up after each push.
6. If an AED arrives, turn it on and follow its voice.
7. **Do not stop** until help takes over, they start breathing, or you are too tired to go on. Swap with a helper every 2 minutes.

Children, babies, drowning and overdose victims do better with breaths too, if you are trained.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Drowning, lightning and cold

**Drowning.** Reach or throw something to the person; do not go in unless you are trained. Once they are out, if you are trained, give 2 rescue breaths, then 30 pushes and 2 breaths, over and over. Not trained? Hands-only CPR is far better than nothing.

**Lightning.** A person struck by lightning carries no electric charge and is **safe to touch**. Most people struck survive. Move to a safer spot if the storm goes on, then start CPR at once if they are not breathing normally. Check for burns and other injuries after.

**Cold.** Someone very cold can look dead: breathing and pulse may be very slow and faint. Handle them gently. Give CPR even if they seem dead, and keep going until they respond or medical help takes over.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

+++

# Choking

Signs: hands at the throat; cannot cough, speak or breathe. If they can cough hard, let them keep coughing.

1. Ask, "Are you choking?" Tell them you will help. Have someone call 911.
2. Stand behind and a little to the side. Hold their chest and lean them forward.
3. Give **5 back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
4. Give **5 abdominal thrusts:** fist just above the belly button, other hand over it, pull sharply in and up.
5. Keep alternating 5 and 5 until the object comes out.
6. If they stop responding, lower them to the ground, make sure 911 is called, and start CPR. Remove any object you can see in the mouth.

Some courses teach abdominal thrusts alone; use what you were taught. Pregnant or very large person: chest thrusts instead. Babies: back blows and chest thrusts, never the belly.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
