# Hypothermia signs

Hypothermia means the body is losing heat faster than it can make it. It can happen **above 40 F (4 C)** when a person is wet, and wind makes it faster.

**Early signs**, often called the umbles:

- shivering
- fumbling hands
- mumbling or slurred speech
- stumbling
- tiredness, poor choices, not caring

**Severe signs:**

- shivering slows or stops while the person is still cold
- very drowsy, confused or hard to wake
- slow, shallow breathing

Act at the first signs. A person with hypothermia often does not notice it. Watch each other, especially when wet, tired or hungry.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

+++

# Treat hypothermia

1. **Get out of the wind and wet** into a shelter, tent or vehicle.
2. **Swap wet clothes for dry ones.** Cut them off if pulling would make the person move a lot.
3. **Insulate from the ground** with a pad, packs or thick dry branches.
4. **Wrap in dry layers** like a sleeping bag or blankets, then wrap a vapor barrier around it all: plastic sheet, tarp or emergency blanket. Cover the head, leaving the face clear.
5. **Warm the center of the body:** warm bottles or heat packs wrapped in cloth on the chest, neck, armpits and groin. Skin-to-skin warmth in a sleeping bag helps if you have nothing else.
6. **Warm, sweet drinks only if fully alert** and able to swallow. Never alcohol. Nothing by mouth if drowsy.

Keep them wrapped and dry after they warm up.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

+++

# Severe hypothermia

Shivering has stopped, or the person is very drowsy or not responding.

1. **Call for rescue now.** Send an SOS.
2. **Handle very gently.** Rough moves can upset the heart's rhythm.
3. **Keep them lying flat.** Do not let them stand or walk.
4. **Warm only the trunk**, as on the last page. Do not warm the arms and legs first.
5. Wrap them fully: insulation under, dry layers, vapor barrier on top.
6. **Check breathing carefully.** It can be very slow and faint, so look and listen for a full minute.
7. Not breathing? Start CPR, even if they seem dead. Keep going until they respond or help takes over.

WARNING: Do NOT rub or massage the arms and legs.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

+++

# Frostbite

**Signs:** numbness; white or grayish-yellow skin; skin that feels firm or waxy. Fingers, toes, nose, ears, cheeks and chin go first.

1. Get out of the cold. Check for hypothermia and treat that first.
2. **Thaw only if it will not freeze again.** Refreezing does more harm than waiting.
3. **Warm, not hot, water**, about 99 to 102 F (37 to 39 C): comfortable to an unhurt hand. Keep it in until the skin is soft, often about 30 minutes. Thawing hurts.
4. No warm water? Use body heat: fingers in the armpits, feet on a companion's belly.
5. **Do not walk on thawed feet** unless you must to get out.
6. Pad between fingers and toes. Cover loosely. Do not break blisters.

WARNING: Do NOT rub or massage frostbitten skin, and do not rub it with snow.

WARNING: Do NOT warm it at a fire, stove or heating pad. Numb skin burns easily.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Immersion foot

Also called trench foot. It comes from feet that stay cold and wet for hours or days, even well above freezing.

**Signs:** red skin, numbness, tingling pain, cramps, swelling, blisters. Later the skin may turn dark purple, blue or gray.

**Treat it:**

1. Take off wet boots and socks.
2. Dry the feet gently and keep them warm and raised.
3. Rest. Walking on damaged feet makes them worse.
4. Get medical care, especially for blisters or dark skin.

**Prevent it:**

- Carry spare socks and change into dry ones every day, or more often when wet.
- Air, dry and warm your feet at least once a day.
- Sleep in dry socks, never in wet boots.
- Loosen tight boots so blood can flow.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
