# Two half hitches
![Half hitch (A) and two half hitches (B)](atp-p206-1)

A quick way to tie a line to a tree or post.

1. Pass the end around the post.
2. Take it around the standing part and back under itself. That is one half hitch.
3. Make a second one the same way.
4. Slide them snug.

One half hitch alone slips.

# Round turn and two half hitches
![Round turn and two half hitches on a pole](atp-p198-1)

Holds a loaded line, such as a tarp ridgeline or a food-bag line, and still unties easily.

1. Take the end around the tree twice, so it circles it fully.
2. Tie two half hitches around the standing part.
3. Pull snug and leave a tail.

The wraps take the pull; the hitches only hold the end.
+++
# Clove hitch
![Clove hitch tied in the middle and at the end of a rope](atp-p208-1)

Quick around a pole, and it starts every lashing.

1. Pass the end around the pole.
2. Cross over the standing part and go around again.
3. Tuck the end under that last wrap.
4. Pull both ends tight.

It can slip if the pole turns or the pull swings. Add a half hitch.
+++
# Timber hitch
![Timber hitch on a log](atp-p207-1)

For dragging logs and poles.

1. Pass the end around the log.
2. Take it around the standing part.
3. Twist it around its own part 3 or more times, not around the standing part.
4. Pull: it tightens.

It falls apart when slack. For a long log, add a half hitch near the front end.
+++
# Prusik
A loop of thin cord wrapped around a thicker line grips when it is pulled and slides when it is loose. Use it for tarp tensioners and adjustable tie-outs.

1. Use cord clearly thinner than the line, about half to two-thirds as thick.
2. Tie the ends into a loop about 12 in (30 cm) long with a double sheet bend. Leave long tails.
3. Lay the loop behind the line.
4. Bring one end around the front and through the other end.
5. Keep wrapping around the line and back through the loop, 3 times in all. Keep the wraps side by side.
6. Pull the free end of the loop straight out to set it.

To move it, push the wraps along the line with no load on the loop. Pull the loop and it grips. Add a wrap if it slips; take one off if it will not slide.

NOTE: For camp rigging only. Never trust it with a person's weight without training.
