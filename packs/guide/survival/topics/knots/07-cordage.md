# Test the fibers
Good cord fibers are long, strong, flexible, and grip each other instead of sliding.

Where to look:
- Inner bark of dead, fallen branches: cedar, elm, basswood. Dead bark gives up its fibers more easily. Young willow shoots also work.
- Long grasses, sedges and rushes. Use these green.
- Stinging nettle stalks. The fibers are in the skin of the stalk. The hairs sting, so wear gloves.
- Surface roots of shrubs and trees with tough bark.
- Your gear: unravel a cotton belt or cloth, or pull the inner strands from a cord.

Test before you work:
1. Pull a length hard.
2. Twist and roll it between your fingers.
3. Tie an overhand knot and tighten it gently.

If it does not snap or cut itself, it will work. Take dead material first, and only what you need.

WARNING: Never use poison ivy or poison oak vines, roots or stems. The oil causes a blistering rash and stays on gloves and cord for years.

# Twist cord
![Twisting two strands into cord](atp-p190-1)

1. Tie or pinch a bundle of fibers at one end.
2. Split it into two even strands.
3. Twist each strand clockwise.
4. Wrap the two around each other counterclockwise. The opposite twists lock.
5. Before a strand thins out, lay in new fibers. Stagger the joins.

Keep strands even; thin spots break first.
+++
# Three-strand braid
![Three-strand braid in four steps](atp-p216-1)

Quick and flat. Good for straps and belts.

1. Tie three strands together at one end.
2. Cross the right strand over the middle.
3. Cross the left strand over the new middle.
4. Repeat, pulling each cross snug.
5. Tie off the end with an overhand knot.
+++
# Whip rope ends
![Whipping the end of a rope](atp-p219-1)

1. Lay a bight of small cord along the rope, sticking out past the end.
2. Wrap tightly toward the end, over the cord.
3. Near the end, tuck the working end through the bight.
4. Pull the other end to draw the bight under. Trim.

Whip or tape both sides before cutting a rope. Synthetic ends can be melted; the hot melt sticks and burns skin.
