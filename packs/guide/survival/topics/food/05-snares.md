# Emergency only

WARNING: Emergency only. Trapping is regulated and usually illegal outside a true survival emergency.

Snares can catch pets and protected animals, and a missed snare leaves an animal suffering. In a short emergency, rest, warmth and signaling usually do more for you than a snare. If you must set them:

- **Set them only away from trails people and pets use**, on small animal runs.
- **Mark every snare** with something bright, and count how many you set.
- **Check them often**, at least morning and evening.
- **Keep nooses small**, for small animals only, and hang them so an animal cannot step into one with a foot.
- **Remove every snare when you leave,** even empty ones.

Handle any catch with gloves or a cloth, and leave alone any animal that looks sick. See **Cleaning and cooking**.
+++
# Simple loop snare
![Simple loop snare at a burrow, with the wire locking loop](atp-p90-1)

Use wire if you have it; cord can slip loose.

1. Make a small eye in the wire end and pass the wire through it.
2. Open a loop just big enough for the animal's head.
3. Hang it over a small run or burrow mouth, propped with twigs.
4. Tie the other end firmly to a stake or tree.

The animal walks in and the loop tightens.
+++
# Drag noose and channel
![Drag noose on a run; double wire loop; channel](atp-p91-1)

Set two forked sticks beside a run and lay a crossbar on them. Hang the noose from the bar where the animal's head will pass, too high to step into. A caught animal pulls the bar loose and it snags in brush.

**Channel:** push sticks in on both sides so the run narrows to just wider than the animal, for about a body length. Stay off the run yourself.
