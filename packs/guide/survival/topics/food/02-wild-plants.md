# Never eat an unknown plant

Many deadly plants look like carrots, parsley, onions or berries. Some can kill within hours.

**There is no safe way to test an unknown plant.** Taste, smell and small bites do not prove anything. Animals eating a plant does not prove it is safe for people.

Only eat a wild plant you know 100%, from real experience gathering it with someone who knows it well. A picture or a description is not enough.

In a short emergency, hunger is very rarely what kills you. Going hungry is far safer than guessing.

WARNING: If someone ate a plant they cannot name, get help now, even if they feel fine. With signal, call Poison Help, 1-800-222-1222. For a seizure, trouble breathing or collapse, call 911 or send an SOS. Do not make them vomit. Keep a piece of the plant.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Deadly lookalikes

**Carrot family.** Flat, umbrella-shaped clusters of tiny flowers, often white, with fernlike or finely divided leaves. Wild carrot and parsnip belong here, and so do two of the deadliest plants in North America:

- **Water hemlock** grows on wet ground: stream banks, ditches, marshes. The root is the most poisonous part. A few bites can cause seizures within an hour and kill.
- **Poison hemlock** is tall, with a hairless, ribbed, hollow stem spotted with purple. Treat every part as deadly. Its sap can also cause a rash, so wear gloves if you must touch it.

Leave the whole carrot family alone.

**Death camas.** Grasslike leaves, a bulb like a wild onion, and clusters of cream-white flowers. All of it is poisonous. Without flowers, its bulbs are easily mistaken for wild onion or edible camas.

Do not eat any wild bulb, root or carrot-like plant. See **Water hemlock and look-alikes** in PACIFIC NORTHWEST.

# Also never eat

- **Wild mushrooms** of any kind. See **Never eat wild mushrooms**.
- **Berries you cannot name,** especially white or yellow ones.
- **Plants with milky sap,** or plants that irritate your skin.
- **Leaves or seeds that smell like almonds.**
- **Fruit that is moldy, rotting or spoiling.**
- **Plants from roadsides, sprayed areas or polluted water.**
- **Spoiled meat or fish.** Cooking does not make it safe.
- **Shellfish from a beach that is closed** or not checked. Shellfish toxins are not destroyed by cooking or freezing, and the food can look, smell and taste normal.

If anything tastes bitter, soapy or burning, even a food you thought you knew, spit it out and rinse your mouth.
