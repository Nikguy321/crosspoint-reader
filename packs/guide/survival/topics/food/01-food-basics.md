# Food can wait

A healthy person can live for weeks without food. Safety, warmth, shelter and water all come first. See **The rule of threes** in EMERGENCY.

Hunger still matters. Without food you get weaker, colder, slower to heal and lower in mood, so plan your meals instead of ignoring them.

- **Eat what you carry.** Count it and split it into small, regular meals so it lasts several days.
- **Do not starve yourself to save food** while you are working hard. A little food now keeps you thinking clearly.
- **Cold weather burns more food.** Eat more of your fat and sugar when it is cold.
- **Help is the goal.** Time spent signaling and staying warm usually does more for you than looking for food.
+++
# Save energy

Every hour of effort costs food you may not have.

- **Rest in the heat of the day.** Do your work in the cool morning and evening.
- **Do not chase animals.** A failed chase costs more energy than a small catch gives back.
- **Eat before you sleep.** A snack, especially one with fat, helps you stay warmer through the night.
- **Stay dry and out of the wind.** Shivering burns energy fast.

**Water short?** Eat little or nothing. Your body uses water to digest food.

- Skip dry, salty or starchy foods and meat; they take more water to digest.
- Fruit and other moist, sweet foods from your own supply need the least water.
- With no water at all, it is better not to eat until you find some.

# Insects and grubs

Many insects are rich in protein and easy to catch with little effort.

**Good choices:** ants, termites, grubs and beetle larvae, grasshoppers and crickets, and earthworms.

**Where to look:** under rotting logs, stones and boards, in grassy fields, and on the ground after rain.

**Avoid:**

- adult insects that bite or sting
- hairy or brightly colored insects, and hairy caterpillars
- anything with a strong, bad smell
- spiders, ticks, flies and mosquitoes
- anything found dead or rotting

**Prepare them:**

1. Pull off legs, wings and heads, and any stinger.
2. Soak earthworms in clean water for several hours, then squeeze out their insides.
3. Cook everything, especially insects with hard shells, which can carry parasites. Roast them, or add them to a stew.

WARNING: If you are allergic to shrimp, crab or lobster, do not eat insects. They can cause the same reaction.
