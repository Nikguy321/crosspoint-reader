# Give wildlife space

- **Stay at least 25 yards (23 m)** from most wildlife, such as deer, elk and moose.
- **Stay at least 100 yards (91 m)** from bears and wolves.
- Some parks require more, such as 50 yards (46 m) from all wildlife.
- If an animal reacts to you, you are too close. Back away slowly.
- **Never feed wild animals,** on purpose or by leaving food out. Fed animals learn to come back and can become dangerous.
- **Never get between a mother and her young.** Any cornered animal will fight.
- **An animal that acts tame, walks up to you, or behaves strangely may be sick.** Keep away and keep pets away from it.

Wash any bite or scratch from a wild animal at once with soap and water, then get to a doctor. See **Stings, bites and rashes** in FIRST AID. For bears and cougars, see PACIFIC NORTHWEST.

# Store food safely

To a bear, anything with a smell is food: meals, trash, toothpaste, sunscreen, bug spray.

- **Cook, eat and store food well away from where you sleep,** at least 100 feet (30 m), and downwind if you can.
- **Never keep food or scented items in your tent** or shelter.
- **Follow local rules;** some areas require a bear-resistant container.
- **Use a hard, bear-resistant container** where you can. Set it on flat ground, away from cliffs and water.
- **Or hang it,** where hanging is allowed: at least 12 feet (3.6 m) up and 5 feet (1.5 m) out from the trunk and the branch.
- **Wash dishes right away.** Do not burn scraps; they still smell.
- **Pack out all trash** and leftover food.

Sealed containers also keep out mice, which can spread disease.

# Small hazards

- **Watch where you put your hands and feet.** Step onto logs and rocks, not blindly over them, and do not reach into holes or under ledges.
- **Leave snakes alone.** Back away slowly and give them room. See **Snakebite** in FIRST AID.
- **Shake out boots, clothes and bedding** before you use them.
- **Check for ticks every day.** Look under the arms, in and around the ears, the belly button, behind the knees, in the hair, between the legs and around the waist.
- **Use insect repellent** with DEET or picaridin on skin, following the label; permethrin is for clothing and gear only.
- **Avoid nests and hives.** Move camp if you find wasps nearby.
- **Do not camp or sleep near rodent nests, burrows or droppings.** Do not sweep up dry droppings; it puts germs in the air.

To remove a tick, see **Ticks** in PACIFIC NORTHWEST. For stings and bites, see FIRST AID.
