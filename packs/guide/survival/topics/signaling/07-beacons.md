# Personal locator beacons

A personal locator beacon (PLB) sends a distress alert and your position by satellite to rescuers. It is one-way: it cannot say what is wrong. Use it only when life or health is at risk.

**Before the trip:** register it with NOAA (free, and the law). Renew every 2 years. Check the battery date. Carry it on your body.

**In an emergency:**

1. Get to open sky, away from cliffs and thick trees.
2. Raise the antenna fully. Keep it pointing up, clear of your body, plants and the ground.
3. Turn it on.
4. **Leave it on** until you are rescued or rescuers tell you to turn it off. The battery lasts at least 24 hours.
5. Keep your other signals ready for the last stretch.

NOTE: Set it off by mistake? Call the U.S. Air Force rescue center at 1-800-851-3051.

# Satellite messaging

Some phones and handheld satellite messengers can send an SOS text by satellite where there is no cell signal.

- **Get open sky.** Leave thick trees, canyon walls and cliff bases. Climb higher if you must. Hold still; a message may take minutes and several tries.
- **Send who, what, where:** how many people, who is hurt and how, your coordinates, what help you need, and what food, water, shelter and battery you have. Keep it plain.
- **Then keep the device on** and answer every question the rescue center sends.
- **Batteries:** start full, carry a spare power pack and keep both warm. Dim the screen and close other apps.

**Before the trip:**

- Learn how your device sends an SOS. Check that any service it needs is active, and send a test message.
- Turn tracking on if it has it.
- Leave a trip plan with someone at home. Agree what an SOS from you means and what they should do if they get one.
