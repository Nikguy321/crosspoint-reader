# Whistle

**Three blasts, rest, then repeat.** Three of anything means "help".

- A whistle carries much farther than a shout and saves your voice and strength.
- Most whistle signals are heard within a few hundred yards. In good conditions a whistle has been heard about 1 mile (1.6 km) away.
- Wind, rain, snow, running water and thick forest deaden sound. Blow from open ground when you can.
- **Blow often**, and every time you hear searchers calling, an engine or a rotor. Then stop and listen for an answer.
- **No whistle?** Bang a stick on a hollow log or two poles together. Shout only in short bursts.

Every person in your group, children too, should carry a whistle on a cord or a pack strap. Teach children: if lost, stay put and blow the whistle.

# Light at night

At night, any light can be seen from far away.

- **Flash SOS:** three short, three long, three short. Pause, then repeat.
- **Strobes** and flashing lights catch the eye best.
- **Wave the light in a big, slow arc** toward an aircraft or searchers' lights. Do not hold a bright beam on a cockpit; sweep it past.
- **A phone light works**, but save the battery. Between tries for a signal, keep it in airplane mode. Signal when you see or hear searchers.
- **Keep spare batteries warm** inside your clothing. Cold drains them fast.
- **A fire** is a bright night signal too. See Signal fires and smoke.

NOTE: Set out your light and whistle before dark, where you can find them by touch.
