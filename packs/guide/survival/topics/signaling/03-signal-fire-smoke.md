# Signal fires

At night, a fire is the best signal you can make.

- **Three fires in a triangle** (or a line), about 80 feet (25 m) apart, mean distress. Alone? One big, bright fire is enough.
- **Build in the open** where trees will not hide the flames: a clearing, a gravel bar, a shore. On snow, build on a platform of green logs so melt water cannot drown it.
- **Get them ready now.** Lay tinder, kindling and fuel, and cover them against rain. Your camp fire can be one of the three.
- **Light them** when you hear or see searchers.

WARNING: A wildfire can trap you and your rescuers. No fire in a fire ban, or in dry or windy weather: use a mirror, ground signs and lights instead. Clear each site to bare soil, away from dry grass and branches. Keep water and a digging tool close. Never leave a fire alone or set a tree alight. Drown it and stir until cool to the touch.

# Smoke by day
![Smoke generator: log platform, kindling, green boughs](fm92-p301-1)

Build a log platform, pile dry kindling on it and cover it with green boughs. Light it when you hear an aircraft.

Green boughs, moss or damp leaves make thick white smoke. It shows best against dark forest on calm, clear days; wind and rain scatter it.

NOTE: Same fire rules as the last page. Never burn plastic, rubber or fuel: the fumes are toxic and fuel can explode.
