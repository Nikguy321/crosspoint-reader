# Ground-to-air code
![Ground-to-air code: V, X, N, Y, arrow](fm92-p308-1)

Search pilots know these symbols:

- **V** need help
- **X** need medical help
- **N** no
- **Y** yes
- **Arrow**: we are going this way

**SOS** or **HELP** spelled out works too. Use **X** only if someone needs medical care.
+++
# Build it big

- **Size:** each line at least 3 feet (1 m) wide and 18 feet (6 m) long. Bigger is better; keep lines about six times as long as wide.
- **Shape:** straight lines and sharp corners.
- **Contrast:** the sign must stand out from the ground.
- **On snow,** stamp out deep lines and fill them with boughs or dark gear. Use one path in and out so your tracks do not blur the sign.
- **On grass or brush,** lay out logs, rocks or gear, or stamp the grass flat.
- **Bright clothing, a tarp or a space blanket** shows well on dark ground. Weigh the edges down with rocks.
- **Shadows:** pile rocks, brush or snow blocks high so they cast shadows.
- **Place:** in the open, on high ground, where it can be seen from every direction. Keep it out of the shade of trees and cliffs.
- **Add a flag:** tie bright cloth to a tall pole. Movement draws the eye.
+++
# Body signals
![Body signals for a pilot who can see you](fm92-p308-2)

Use these only when the pilot is close enough to see you clearly. Stand in the open.

- **Both arms up:** pick us up.
- **Lying flat:** need medical help urgently.
- **One arm up:** all OK, do not wait.

Never wave one arm to say hello: waving one arm overhead means "all OK".
