# Smoke harms

Wildfire smoke irritates the eyes, nose, throat and lungs. It can drift far from the fire and stay in the air for days.

**Signs:**

- stinging eyes, sore throat
- cough, wheeze, short breath
- headache, unusual tiredness
- chest pain or a fast, pounding heartbeat

**Higher risk:** children, older adults, pregnant people, and anyone with asthma or other lung disease, heart disease, diabetes or kidney disease.

WARNING: Call 911 or send an SOS for chest pain, severe trouble breathing or fainting.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Protect yourself

- **If a fire is near, leave the area.** Follow any evacuation order. If you are trapped, call 911 and give your location.
- Move away from the fire and out of the path of the smoke, not downwind of it. Never go uphill of a fire (WEATHER, Near a wildfire).
- **Cut hard work.** Rest often in the cleanest air you can find.
- Wear a **NIOSH-approved N95 or P100 respirator.** Both straps on, pressed tight to the face with no gaps. A beard breaks the seal.
- Cloth masks, bandanas and surgical masks do **not** filter smoke.
- A respirator does not block carbon monoxide or other gases. Headache, dizziness or nausea near heavy smoke: get to fresh air.
- Respirators make you hotter. Take breaks and drink water.
- With heart or lung disease, ask a doctor before relying on one.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
