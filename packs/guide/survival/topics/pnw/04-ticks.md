# Northwest ticks

- **Western blacklegged tick:** along the Pacific coast. Can carry Lyme disease, anaplasmosis and hard tick relapsing fever.
- **Rocky Mountain wood tick:** in the drier inland mountains, 4,000 to 10,500 ft (1,200 to 3,200 m). Can carry Rocky Mountain spotted fever, Colorado tick fever and tularemia.

**Prevent bites:**

- Wear light-colored long sleeves and long pants tucked into socks or boots.
- Use an EPA-registered repellent on skin, such as DEET or picaridin.
- Treat clothes and gear with 0.5% permethrin. Never put it on skin.
- Check your whole body every day: hair, scalp, ears, armpits, groin, behind the knees.
- Shower within 2 hours of coming in.

**Tick paralysis,** mostly in young children: weakness that starts in the legs and climbs. Find the tick, often hidden in scalp or neck hair, and remove it. Untreated, it can stop breathing: call 911 or send an SOS if breathing or swallowing weakens.

# Remove a tick

1. Use clean, fine-tipped tweezers. Grip the tick **as close to the skin as you can.**
2. **Pull straight up with steady, even pressure.** Do not twist or jerk.
3. If mouth parts break off, lift them out with the tweezers if you can. If not, leave them; the skin pushes them out as it heals.
4. Clean the bite and your hands with soap and water or rubbing alcohol.
5. Seal the tick in a bag or tape. Never crush it with your fingers.
6. **Write down the date** and where you were.
7. Check the rest of your body for more ticks.

WARNING: Do NOT use heat, petroleum jelly or nail polish to make a tick let go. It can force infected tick fluid into the skin.

**See a doctor** for a rash or fever within several weeks of a bite. Say when and where it happened.
