# Water hemlock

![Umbrella-shaped flower clusters, typical of the carrot family](atp-p79-1)

One of the most violently poisonous plants in North America. Grows in wet ground: stream banks, ditches, marshes. Small white flowers in umbrella-shaped clusters. The thick root base has chambers holding a straw-colored or brown poison liquid. All parts are poisonous, the root most. Seizures can start within an hour.

# Other deadly plants

- **Poison hemlock:** 2 to 10 ft (0.6 to 3 m) tall, hollow stems with purple blotches, white umbrella-shaped flowers, a rank smell when crushed. Common on roadsides and open ground.
- **Death camas:** grass-like leaves, cream-white flowers, an onion-like bulb. Easily mistaken for wild onion or edible camas bulbs. All parts are poisonous.
- **Foxglove:** tall spikes of bell-shaped purple, pink or white flowers in meadows, clearings and along streams. All parts poison the heart.

**Rules:**

- Never eat any wild plant that looks like carrot, parsley, parsnip or celery.
- Never eat a wild bulb.
- Smell and taste are not safe tests.
- Do not handle cut water hemlock roots with bare hands. Wash after touching these plants.

WARNING: If anyone eats one of these, call 911 or send an SOS at once. With signal, also call Poison Help, 1-800-222-1222. Keep a piece of the plant to show.
