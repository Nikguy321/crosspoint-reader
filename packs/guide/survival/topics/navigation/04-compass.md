# Compass parts

- **Needle:** the red end points to magnetic north.
- **Housing (dial):** the ring marked N, E, S, W and 0 to 360 degrees. It turns.
- **Orienting arrow:** the arrow drawn inside the housing. It turns with the dial.
- **Direction-of-travel arrow:** on the clear base. Point it where you want to go.
- **Index line:** where the travel arrow meets the dial. Read bearings here.

**Keep it away from metal and electronics.** Phones, radios, batteries, knives, belt buckles, car bodies and power lines all pull the needle. Hold the compass level, at waist height, a step away from anything metal.

A bearing is a direction in degrees, counted clockwise from north: 0 or 360 is north, 90 east, 180 south, 270 west.

NOTE: Practice at home before you need it. A compass you have never used is hard to trust when you are scared.
+++
# Take and follow a bearing

1. Hold the compass level and point the travel arrow at your landmark.
2. Turn the housing until the red needle sits inside the orienting arrow ("red in the shed").
3. Read the bearing at the index line. Write it down.
4. To walk it, keep the needle in the arrow and look where the travel arrow points.
5. Pick a landmark on that line, put the compass away, and walk to it.
6. At the landmark, check again and pick the next one.

**To go back the way you came,** add 180 to your bearing (or subtract 180 if it is over 180). From 70, the way back is 250.

Do not walk staring at the compass. You will drift. Walk from landmark to landmark instead.
+++
# Declination

**Magnetic north is not true north.** The needle points to magnetic north; maps are drawn to true north. The angle between them is the **declination**. The map margin shows it, with the year it was measured. On an old map that number can be several degrees off.

**Pacific Northwest:** about 12 to 16 degrees east in 2026, shrinking about 1 degree every 8 to 10 years. Look up the current value (NOAA declination calculator) before a trip.

**If your compass has a declination setting,** set it once, and map and compass bearings will match.

**If not, correct by hand** (for east declination):

- Map to compass: **subtract** the declination. Map bearing 90, minus 15, set 75.
- Compass to map: **add** it. Compass bearing 75, plus 15, plot 90.

WARNING: Fifteen degrees off is a big miss: about 1,400 feet (430 m) to one side for every mile you walk.
+++
# Needle compass

No compass? Make a rough one.

1. Stroke a steel needle or pin many times, **in one direction only**, with one end of a magnet (a small speaker has one). Lift the magnet away after each stroke. Rubbing on silk or hair, as old manuals say, does little.
2. Wipe the needle through your hair to oil it, so it floats.
3. Lay it on a leaf, a chip of bark or a scrap of cork floating in still water, sheltered from wind. A needle on a thread works too.
4. It turns slowly and settles pointing north-south.

**Which end is north?** The needle cannot tell you. Check against the sun or a shadow-tip line. In the morning the sun is in the east, in the afternoon in the west.

It is rough and fades: stroke it again often and keep it away from metal.
