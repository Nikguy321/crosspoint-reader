# Hill
![A hill and its contour rings](atp-p172-2)

**Closed contour rings, one inside another.** The smallest ring is the top. From there the ground slopes down in every direction.

A hilltop gives a wide view, a better chance of phone signal, and a place to be seen. Rings packed close on one side mean that side is steep.
+++
# Saddle
![A saddle between two high points](atp-p173-1)

**A low point between two high points**, often shaped like an hourglass on the map. Ground rises on two opposite sides and falls on the other two.

A saddle is the natural pass: the easiest way across a ridge. It is also a good landmark, easy to spot on the map and on the ground.
+++
# Ridge
![A ridge line and its contours](atp-p174-1)

**A long line of high ground.** The U or V shapes of the contours point away from the high ground: downhill.

A ridge is often easier walking than a brushy valley, with better views and signal. Leave ridges and peaks when thunder or lightning is near.
+++
# Valley
![A valley and its stream](atp-p173-2)

**Low ground between high ground, usually with a stream.** The U or V shapes of the contours point uphill: upstream.

Valleys have water but often thick brush. Following a stream downhill can lead to waterfalls and cliffs. Look at the map before you commit.
+++
# Cliff
![Contour lines crowded into a cliff](atp-p176-1)

**Contour lines squeezed together, touching, or merged into one line with tick marks** pointing to the low side.

Plan your route around cliffs. Never walk toward one at night or in fog. If a descent keeps getting steeper, climb back up and find another way down.
