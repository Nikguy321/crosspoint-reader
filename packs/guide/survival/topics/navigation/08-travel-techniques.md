# Pace yourself

**Slow and steady wins.** The slowest person sets the pace. Keep everyone in sight and check on each other often.

- **Short steps uphill.** Keep your weight over your feet and set each sole flat.
- **Rest briefly and often.** Long stops chill you and make starting again hard.
- **Adjust layers** before you sweat: take one off as you warm up, put it back on when you stop.
- **Drink and snack** a little, often, even when not hungry or thirsty.
- **Look back** often, so you will know the way if you must return.
- **Travel in daylight only.** Stop early enough to make shelter, gather firewood and find water before dark.
- **Watch each other** for signs of cold, heat or exhaustion.

Look before you put a hand or foot anywhere.

If the weather or the ground gets worse, turn around. Pushing on is how small problems become emergencies.

# Rough ground

- **Steep slopes:** zigzag up rather than climbing straight. Going down, keep your back straight and knees bent.
- **Loose rock (scree):** go around if you can. Coming down, take short, shuffling steps, and stay close together so a loose rock cannot hit someone below at speed.
- **Big rocks (talus):** step on top of each rock and on its uphill side.
- **Wet or mossy rock, logs and steep grass** are very slippery. Use brush for balance only; never pull on it.
- **Dense brush:** go around it. Ridges and open forest are often easier than brushy valley floors.
- **Snow:** stay off and below slopes steeper than about 30 degrees, overhanging snow edges (cornices) and gullies. Danger is highest after heavy snow or rain, on fast-warming days, and where snow cracks or collapses. Check the avalanche forecast first.

WARNING: Lightning: no place outside is safe. Get off peaks and ridges, away from lone trees; a tent does not protect you. See WEATHER.
