# Phone GPS works without signal

**GPS satellites only send; your phone only listens.** It can find your position with no cell service, even in airplane mode on most phones. Without service the first fix can take several minutes: stand in the open and wait.

**But the map needs downloading first.** Without a saved map you get a dot on a blank screen. Before the trip, download offline maps of the area plus a wide margin.

**Find your coordinates:** a map or compass app shows latitude and longitude. Write them down, and read them out first on any call for help.

**Save the battery:**

- Airplane mode, screen dim. Check, then turn it off, unless rescuers asked you to keep it on.
- Carry a spare battery pack and cable.
- Cold drains batteries fast. Keep phone and spare in an inside pocket, and in your sleeping bag at night.
- Keep it dry in a plastic bag.

NOTE: Before you need it, learn how your phone shows coordinates and how to drop a pin.

# Do not trust it blindly

Under open sky, a phone is usually right to about 16 feet (5 m). **Near cliffs, in deep canyons and under heavy trees** the signal is blocked or bounces off rock, and the dot can wander well off your real spot. Step into the open, wait a minute, and check it against what you see.

**Phones die, break, get wet and get lost.** Carry a paper map and a compass, and know how to use them.

**Write it down on paper:**

- coordinates of camp, car and trailhead
- water and junctions you pass
- your bearings and the time you left each place

**Save points in the phone too,** so it can point you back.

**Check the format.** Coordinates come in different styles, such as 40.2500 or 40 15.00. Say which style you are reading. In North America, longitude is **west**, often shown with a minus sign. Never drop it.
