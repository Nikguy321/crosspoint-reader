# Pick the crossing

**If you are unsure, do not cross.** Wait, turn back, or find another way. No crossing is worth your life.

Look from high ground first, up and down the stream. Choose a spot that is:

- **wide, shallow and slow**, on a straight stretch, or where it splits into several small channels
- **gravel bottomed**, with an easy, low bank on the far side
- **well above** rapids, falls, logjams and fallen trees, so a fall does not carry you into them

**Do not cross water above your knees,** judged by the shortest person in the group. Six inches (15 cm) of fast water can knock you down.

**Snowmelt and glacier streams** are lowest in the early morning and rise through warm afternoons. After heavy rain, wait for the water to drop.

WARNING: Mountain streams are often very cold. Do not linger in the water, and get dry and warm right after. See the PACIFIC NORTHWEST section on cold water.
+++
# Cross with a pole

1. Put your phone, fire kit and a warm layer in a waterproof bag on your body.
2. **Unbuckle your pack's hip belt and chest strap** so you can throw it off if you fall.
3. **Keep shoes on.** With no spare pair, take out socks and insoles and cross in your boots.
4. Find a stout pole or stick about as tall as you are.
5. **Face upstream.** Plant the pole on the upstream side and lean on it slightly.
6. Shuffle sideways, angling a little downstream. Move one foot or the pole at a time; keep two points firmly down.
7. Look at the far bank, not the rushing water, which can make you dizzy.
8. If it gets deeper or faster than you planned, go back the way you came.
+++
# Group crossing and falling in

**Together:** link arms or hold each other's pack straps, side by side, with the **strongest person upstream** to break the current. Take short steps together and call out each move.

**If you are swept away:**

- Shed your pack if it drags you down.
- **Float on your back, feet downstream, toes up at the surface.** Use your feet to push off rocks.
- **Do not stand up in fast water.** A foot caught between rocks can pin you under.
- Keep away from logjams and fallen trees. Water flows through them; people do not.
- Swim at an angle toward the shore, to slow or shallow water, then crawl out.

**Afterward,** get out of wet clothes and warm up at once, even if you feel fine.
