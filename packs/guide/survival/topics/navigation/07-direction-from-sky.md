# Shadow-tip method
![Mark the shadow tip twice, then face north](fm92-p292-1)

1. Push a stick upright. Mark its shadow tip.
2. Wait 15 minutes. Mark the tip again.
3. First mark is **west**, second is **east**.
4. First mark on your left: you face **north**.

Works anywhere on a sunny day.
+++
# Equal-shadow method
![Morning and afternoon shadows touch the same arc](atp-p163-1)

More accurate, but takes hours.

1. In the morning, mark the shadow tip of an upright stick.
2. Draw an arc around the stick through that mark, using a cord.
3. Near midday the shadow shrinks, then grows.
4. Mark where it touches the arc again in the afternoon.

The first mark is west, the second east.
+++
# Watch method
![Point the hour hand at the sun](atp-p164-1)

**Northern half of the world:** hold an analog watch flat. Point the hour hand at the sun. Halfway between it and 12 is **south**.

**Southern half:** point 12 at the sun. Halfway to the hour hand is **north**.

On daylight saving time, use 1 in place of 12.

No watch? Draw a clock face. Rough only, worst near the equator.
+++
# North Star
![Big Dipper, Cassiopeia and the North Star](atp-p165-1)

Find the Big Dipper. The two stars at the outer edge of its cup are the **pointers**. Follow them about five times their gap to a lone, medium-bright star: the **North Star**. It is within about 1 degree of true north.

**Cassiopeia**, a W of five stars, sits opposite the Dipper, with the North Star between them.
+++
# Southern Cross
![Southern Cross, pointer stars and the south line](atp-p166-1)

**Southern half of the world only.** Find the Southern Cross: four bright stars in a cross. Extend its long axis about 4.5 times its length toward the horizon. Below that point is **south**.

Two bright **pointer stars** beside it confirm the line and tell it apart from the False Cross.
+++
# Moon and night checks

**A rough check only.** The moon's lit side faces the sun, even after sunset.

- If the moon rises **before sunset**, its lit side faces **west**.
- If it rises **after midnight**, its lit side faces **east**.

**Bright moonlight** casts shadows. On a clear night near full moon, the shadow-tip method works with the moon instead of the sun.

**Stars move** across the sky through the night. Any star near the horizon drifts. Check your direction again every 15 minutes or so, or switch to a new star.

WARNING: Do not trust moss to find north. Moss grows on every side of trees, wherever it is damp and shaded.
