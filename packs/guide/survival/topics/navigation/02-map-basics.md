# Orient the map

A map is easiest to read when it lies flat with its north pointing north. Then everything on the map lines up with the ground around you.

**With a compass:**

1. Set the compass dial to N (360). If the compass is not set for declination, use 360 minus the east declination: 345 for 15 degrees east.
2. Lay the compass edge along the map's side edge or a north-south line, travel arrow toward the top of the map.
3. Turn the map and compass together until the red needle sits inside the orienting arrow.

**Without a compass:** find a feature you can see that is also on the map, like a ridge, a lake or a road. Turn the map until the feature on the map points at the real one. Check with a second feature; one alone can leave the map upside down.

Keep the map oriented as you walk. Turn the map, not your head.

# Map scale

**The scale** tells how much ground one unit on the map covers. At 1:24,000, 1 inch on the map is 24,000 inches on the ground: 2,000 feet, about 0.4 mile (1 cm = 240 m). About 2.6 inches is a mile.

**The bar scale** in the margin is a printed ruler. Right of zero it counts whole units; left of zero it splits one unit into small parts. Most maps have bars for miles, feet and kilometers.

**Contour lines** join points of the same height. The margin gives the **contour interval**: the height between two lines, such as 20 or 40 feet. Every fifth line is darker, usually labeled with its height.

- Lines close together: steep ground.
- Lines far apart: gentle ground.
- Count the lines to judge how much you will climb.

NOTE: Check the date on the map. Trails, roads and streams change; ridges and valleys do not.

# Measure distance
![Tick a paper strip, read it on the bar scale](atp-p177-1)

1. Lay a straight paper edge so it touches both points.
2. Tick the paper at each point.
3. Move the paper to the bar scale and read the distance.

For a winding trail or stream, tick off one short straight piece at a time, pivoting the paper at each tick. Trail distance is longer than straight-line distance.
