# Usually, stay

**First, call for help:** call or text 911, or send an SOS from a satellite device. See EMERGENCY.

**Searchers start where you were last seen.** Every mile you walk makes the search bigger. A person who stays put is much easier to find.

Stay where you are if:

- someone knows your plan and when you are due back
- you or someone with you is hurt
- the weather is bad, or night is coming
- you are not sure which way help is

Do not decide in the first shock. Sit, drink, eat, and let your head clear.

Then use the time well:

1. Get out of wind and wet; build a shelter before dark.
2. Find water close by.
3. Make signals that can be seen from the air and heard from the ground.
4. Rest between jobs.

NOTE: A car, tent or bright gear is far easier to spot from the air than a person walking under trees. Stay near it.

# When to move

Federal park guidance: move only if staying is a real threat to life. That usually means:

- **Your spot is dangerous:** rising water, rockfall, fire or avalanche ground. Go a short way to safety, then stop.
- **There is no water** close by. Search in short trips, marking your way, and come back to your spot.
- **No one knows to look,** days have passed with no sign of a search, and you **know exactly where you are** and a safe way out, with the water, food and clothing for the trip.

Before you go:

1. Leave a note at the spot: the time you left, your direction, your route and how you are.
2. Mark your way as you go: arrows of sticks or rocks, at every turn.
3. Stay together. If someone must go for help, send two people. Going alone is dangerous.

Travel in daylight only. Stop early enough to make camp before dark. If things get worse, turn back to your last safe spot.
