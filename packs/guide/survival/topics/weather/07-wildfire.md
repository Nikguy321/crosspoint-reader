# Get out early

**Signs of fire nearby:** the smell of smoke, a smoke column, falling ash, or a glow at night.

1. **Leave early.** Do not wait to see the flames. Fire can move faster than you can walk.
2. **Go away from the fire** and away from the way it is moving. Wind pushes fire and throws embers ahead of it.
3. **Never climb a slope above a fire.** Fire travels uphill fast.
4. **Head for a road, a trailhead, or open ground** with little to burn.
5. **Report it.** Call 911 or send an SOS: where the fire is, which way it is moving, and where you are.
6. **In smoke, wear an N95 respirator** if you have one. A cloth or bandana does not filter smoke.
7. **Obey fire bans, closures and evacuation orders.**

See **Wildfire smoke** for smoke and health.

# If trapped

1. **Call 911 or send an SOS** with your location. Help may be slow or unable to reach you. At night, turn on a light so rescuers can see you.
2. **Go to the spot with the least to burn** that you can reach without going uphill toward the fire: bare rock or gravel, a bare clearing or green meadow that will not burn, or ground already burned out, with nothing left to burn.
3. **Get as low as you can**, such as in a ditch or a hollow.
4. **Lie face down.**
5. **Cover your body** with dirt, mud or water, or with a fabric that does not burn easily, such as a wool blanket. Avoid synthetics.
6. **Cover your nose and mouth** with an N95 or a cloth, and stay calm. Smoke is often a bigger danger than flames.

WARNING: Do NOT try to outrun a fire uphill. In a car caught by fire, stay inside with the windows closed and the fans off.
