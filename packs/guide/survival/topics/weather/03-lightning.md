# When thunder roars, go indoors

**No place outside is safe in a thunderstorm.** If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck, even under blue sky. Lightning can strike 10 to 15 miles (16 to 24 km) from the storm.

**Safe places:** a solid building with wiring or plumbing, or a hard-topped metal car or truck with the windows up.

**Not safe:** tents, picnic shelters, sheds, rock overhangs, and under trees.

1. **Plan around storms.** In the mountains they often build by early afternoon. Start early and be off peaks and ridges by noon.
2. **Move at the first warning:** towering clouds, a darkening sky, rising wind. Do not wait for the first flash.
3. **Get to safety** as soon as you hear thunder.
4. **Stay there 30 minutes** after the last thunder you hear.

+++

# Caught outside

You are not safe, but you can lower the risk. **Keep moving toward a building, a vehicle, or lower ground** (not a creek bed or dry wash).

- **Get off peaks, ridges, hilltops and open fields** at once.
- **Never shelter under a lone tree** or near any tall, isolated object. In a forest, stay among the shorter trees.
- **Never shelter under a cliff or rock overhang.**
- **Get out of and away from water.**
- **Stay away from metal and wet things**: fences, power lines, wet ropes.
- **Spread out a group**, at least 50 feet (15 m) apart, so one strike cannot hurt everyone.
- **Never lie flat** on the ground.

NOTE: Some guides teach a crouch. NWS says it does not make you safer; keep moving to safer ground.

A person struck by lightning carries no charge and is safe to touch. Call for help and start CPR if they are not breathing normally. See **CPR and choking**.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
