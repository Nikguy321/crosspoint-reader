# High clouds: a front is coming

Watch the sky change over a day.

1. **Cirrus**: thin, white, wispy streaks very high up. Often the first sign that a warm front is on the way.
2. **Cirrostratus**: the streaks spread into a thin, milky veil over the whole sky. A **halo** (ring) may show around the sun or moon.
3. **Altostratus**: the veil thickens into a gray sheet. The sun looks dim and blurred, or disappears.
4. **Lower and darker**: the gray layer drops and thickens until it rains or snows.

When high clouds keep **thickening and lowering**, expect rain or snow within a day or so. Use the time: pick a sheltered camp, gather dry fuel, and get your rain gear on top of your pack.

High wisps that stay thin and patchy, and then fade, usually mean no change.

+++

# Tall towers: thunderstorms

**Cumulus** are puffy white clouds with flat bottoms. Small ones that stay small mean fair weather.

On warm, humid days, watch them by late morning. If they keep **growing taller**, like towers or cauliflower, a storm is building.

A **cumulonimbus** is the full thunderstorm cloud: very tall and dark at the base, often with a flat, spreading top shaped like an anvil. It brings:

- lightning
- hail
- sudden strong, gusty wind
- heavy rain and flash floods

**When you see towers growing, darkening skies or rising wind:**

1. Get off peaks, ridges and open high ground now, before the first thunder.
2. Head for a building or a hard-topped vehicle, or lower ground.
3. Stay out of dry washes and creek beds.

See **Lightning** and **Flash floods**.

+++

# Low gray layers and lens clouds

**Stratus** is a flat, even gray layer, low over the land, like fog that has lifted. It may bring drizzle or light snow, and often means little change for the next few hours.

**Nimbostratus** is a thick, dark gray layer that hides the sun and brings **steady rain or snow**, often for hours. Being wet and cold for hours leads to hypothermia. Make camp, get dry and stay warm. See **Hypothermia and frostbite**.

**Ragged low clouds racing past** on the wind mean the bad weather is not over.

Low clouds can hide peaks and landmarks. Fix your position on the map before they close in.

**Lens clouds**: smooth, saucer-shaped clouds that sit still over a peak or downwind of a ridge. They mark **strong winds up high**. Expect strong, gusty wind on ridges and on the downwind side of the mountain. They are not a rain sign on their own.
