# Before you go

The best weather sign is an official forecast. Signs in the sky only warn you a few hours to a day ahead.

- **Check the forecast** the day before and again the morning you leave. Look for watches and warnings: thunderstorm, flash flood, winter storm, wind chill, heat.
- **Carry a weather radio.** NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts official warnings 24 hours a day on seven VHF channels, 162.400 to 162.550 MHz. It needs a radio with a weather band. Many have an alert that sounds only for your area. It can work where phones have no signal, though not everywhere.
- **Know the sunset time.** Pick a turnaround time that gets you back or into camp with daylight to spare.
- **In the mountains, start early.** Thunderstorms often build by early afternoon. Plan to be off peaks and ridges by noon.
- **Tell someone your plan** and when you will be back.

If the forecast is bad, change the plan before you start.

# Bad weather coming

These clues can mean a change is coming. Act early: head for camp, lower ground or shelter before it hits.

- **Wind shifts fast or grows stronger.** Drop a little dry grass to see the direction. A sudden shift means unsettled weather.
- **A ring (halo) around the sun or moon.** It comes from thin, high ice cloud that often moves in ahead of a front. Rain or snow may follow within a day or so.
- **High, thin clouds thicken and drop lower,** turning into a gray sheet. See **Clouds that warn**.
- **Puffy clouds grow tall by midday,** or clouds keep growing on the upwind side of a mountain.
- **Campfire smoke sinks low** or flattens out instead of rising.
- **Sounds carry far and smells grow strong** in heavy, still, damp air.
- **Birds and insects fly low.**
- **A red sky at sunrise** can signal a storm on the way.

# Fair weather signs

These clues point to settled weather for the next several hours:

- **Heavy dew or frost in the morning.** It forms after a clear night with little wind.
- **Morning fog in valleys that burns off** by mid-morning. This kind of fog forms on clear, calm nights.
- **Smoke rising straight up** in a thin column.
- **A clear night full of bright stars.**
- **Small, puffy white clouds with flat bottoms** that stay small through the afternoon.
- **A red sky at sunset,** as the sky to the west clears.

NOTE: Sayings and signs are clues, not forecasts. Mountains make their own weather, and one sign alone proves nothing. Look for several signs that agree, and trust an official forecast or warning over any saying.
