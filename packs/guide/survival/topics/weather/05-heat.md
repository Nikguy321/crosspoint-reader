# Beat the heat

- **Travel early and late.** Rest in shade from about 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.
- **Cover up loosely**: light-colored clothes, long sleeves, a hat with a 3-inch (8 cm) brim. Soak them when you pass water.
- **Drink often**, before you feel thirsty, and **eat salty snacks**. Lots of water without food can be dangerous.

**Sunburn** happens even on cloudy or freezing days, faster at altitude or near snow and water. Use broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or more. Reapply at least every 2 hours, and after sweating or swimming.

**Snow blindness** is sunburned eyes from glare off snow or water: gritty, painful, teary eyes that hurt in light. Wear sunglasses labeled 100% UV or UV400, or cardboard or bark with thin eye slits. Rest with the eyes covered until it eases. Do not rub them.

WARNING: Confusion or odd behavior in the heat means heat stroke. Call for help; cool them fast. See **Heat exhaustion and heat stroke**.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.
