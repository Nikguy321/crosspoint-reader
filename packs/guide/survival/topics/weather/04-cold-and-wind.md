# Wind chill

**Wind chill** is how cold it feels on your skin, and it shows how fast skin can freeze.

**Example from NWS:** air at 0 F (-18 C) with a 15 mph (24 km/h) wind gives a wind chill of -19 F (-28 C). Bare skin can freeze in about **30 minutes**. Colder air or stronger wind freezes it faster.

- **Cover all skin**: a hat, a scarf or mask over face and mouth, and mittens (warmer than gloves).
- **Wear loose layers** under a tightly woven, water-repellent outer layer. See **Clothing: your first shelter**.
- **Stay dry.** Wet clothing chills you fast, and wind makes it worse.
- **Work slowly** and open layers before you sweat.
- **Get out of the wind** behind trees, rocks or a snow wall.
- **Watch each other.** Shivering, fumbling hands, slurred speech or confusion point to hypothermia. Numb, white or grayish, waxy patches on faces, ears or fingers point to frostbite.

See **Hypothermia and frostbite**.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Winter storms

In a **blizzard**, gusts reach 35 mph (56 km/h) or more and blowing snow cuts what you can see to under 1/4 mile (400 m). In a **whiteout**, snow and sky blend; you cannot see slopes, drop-offs or landmarks.

1. **Check the forecast.** If a winter storm is coming, stay home or turn back early.
2. **Do not travel in a whiteout.** Stop and shelter early, while you still have daylight and strength.
3. **Mark your route** and fix your position on the map before it closes in. Mark your shelter with something bright.
4. **Get out of the wind and stay dry.** Brush off snow; change wet socks. See **Snow shelters**.
5. **Eat and drink often.** Warm, sweet drinks help; skip alcohol and caffeine. Treat melted snow before drinking it. See **Making water safe**.

# Sheltering from a storm

**Stuck in a car? Stay inside it.** Tie a bright cloth to the antenna as a signal.

1. Run the engine and heater about 10 minutes each hour, with one window open a crack.
2. Keep the exhaust pipe clear of snow. A blocked pipe fills the car with carbon monoxide.
3. Wrap up, head included, and huddle together.
4. Keep moving your arms and legs, and stay awake.

**In a tent or snow shelter,** keep a vent hole open and clear it of snow.

WARNING: Never burn a stove, heater, candle or lantern inside a tent or snow shelter. Carbon monoxide has no smell and can kill you in your sleep. Cook outside.
