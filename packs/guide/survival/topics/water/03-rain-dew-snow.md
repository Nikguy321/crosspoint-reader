# Catch rain

Rain is often the cleanest water you can find, but it can still carry germs and chemicals from the air and from whatever it runs over.

- Spread a tarp, poncho, emergency blanket or plastic sheet between trees or sticks. Make one corner the low point and set a container under it.
- Shape the sheet into a funnel. A stick or stone under the middle stops a sag from pooling water you cannot reach.
- Let the first few minutes of rain rinse off dust, salt and droppings, then start collecting.
- Do not collect off painted, dyed, oily or chemically treated material.
- Rain also collects in rock basins, tree hollows and large leaves.
- Store water in covered containers. Do not pour untreated rain into water you have already treated.

Treat rainwater before you drink it. See **Making water safe**.

NOTE: Some old manuals say fresh rainwater needs no treatment. Current CDC advice is to treat it.

# Collect dew

Dew forms on clear, calm nights and dries soon after sunrise, so start early.

- **Wipe it up.** Mop dew off grass, leaves, rocks, metal and tarps with an absorbent cloth or sponge. Wring it into a container. Repeat.
- **Walk it up.** Tie cloth or tufts of fine grass around your ankles and walk through wet grass, then wring them out. This can gather 1 quart (1 L) or more.
- **Set out a sheet.** A tarp or plastic sheet left out overnight collects dew to wipe up at dawn.

Avoid poisonous plants and fields that may have been sprayed with chemicals. Treat dew water like any other water.

# Melt snow and ice
![Water machine: a cloth bag of snow by a fire](atp-p58-2)

Melt it first. Use clean snow; ice gives more water, faster. Start with a little water in the pot, then add snow bit by bit. A cloth bag of snow hung near the fire drips into a container. No fire? Carry snow in a bottle under a clothing layer, not on bare skin. Treat melted water.

WARNING: Do NOT eat snow or ice. It chills you and costs body heat. <!-- myth-ok -->
