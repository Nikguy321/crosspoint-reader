# How much water you need

Resting in mild weather, about 68 F (20 C), an adult needs about 2 to 3 quarts (2 to 3 L) of water a day. Heat, cold, altitude, hard work and illness raise that a lot. Heavy sweating can lose 1 quart (1 L) or more an hour.

- **Drink small amounts often.** Working in heat, take about 1 cup (250 mL) every 15 to 20 minutes.
- **Do not wait until you are very thirsty,** but do not force down water either. Drinking more than about 1.5 quarts (1.4 L) an hour, hour after hour, can make your blood salt dangerously low.
- **In cold weather** you feel less thirsty but still lose water.
- **Sweating for hours?** Eat salty snacks with your water.
- **Ration sweat, not water.** Work in the cool hours, rest in shade, keep clothes on, and sip the water you have instead of saving it.
- **Water short?** Eat less. Digesting food uses water.

# Signs of dehydration

Watch yourself and others for:

- thirst
- dark yellow urine, or peeing less often
- headache
- dizziness or feeling lightheaded
- tiredness, weakness or muscle cramps
- nausea

What to do:

1. Stop and rest in shade, out of the wind.
2. Sip water often. If you have sweated hard, add a salty snack or a sports drink.
3. Do not drink alcohol, and avoid very sugary drinks.
4. Keep going only when urine is lighter and comes more often.

Get help if signs get worse or last more than an hour.

WARNING: Confusion, odd behavior, fainting, a seizure, repeated vomiting or a very hot body is an emergency. It can be heat stroke, or low blood salt from too much plain water. Call 911 or send an SOS. Cool a very hot person fast with water and shade. Do not push plain water on a confused person. See FIRST AID.
