# Ground solar still
![Below-ground solar still](atp-p65-1)

Sun heats the pit; water from damp soil or cut green plants condenses on clear plastic and drips into a cup. Salt or dirty water poured in a trench beside the pit works too.

At best about 1 pint to 1 quart (0.5 to 1 L) a day, often less. One person needs three or more. Dig in cool hours so you do not sweat away more than you gain.
+++
# Building a ground still

You need a container, clear plastic about 6 feet (2 m) square, and a stone.

1. Pick damp soil in full sun all day, such as a dry streambed.
2. Dig a bowl 3 feet (1 m) across and 2 feet (60 cm) deep, with a small pit in the center for the container.
3. Set the container in. Tubing from its bottom up over the rim lets you drink without opening the still.
4. Line the slopes with cut green plants you know are safe, if you have them.
5. Cover the hole with the plastic, soil piled on its edges.
6. Set the stone in the middle so the plastic sags into a cone 16 inches (40 cm) deep, point over the container, not touching the soil sides.
7. Seal the edges with more soil. Plug the tube between drinks.
8. For salt or dirty water, dig a trench 10 inches (25 cm) deep and 10 inches from the rim. Fill it without spilling near the plastic.

# Vegetation bag still

Fill a clear plastic bag half to three-quarters with green leafy plants, no sharp sticks. Add a stone, scoop air in, tie it shut. Lay it on a sunny slope, mouth uphill, stone at the low corner. Drain it before sunset; change the leaves daily. Expect about 1 1/4 cups (300 mL) a day. Treat it if you can.

WARNING: Use only plants you know are not poisonous.
+++
# Transpiration bag
![Clear bag tied over a leafy branch](atp-p64-1)

Tie a clear plastic bag over a leafy branch of a living tree or bush in full sun. Seal it tight around the branch, a stone in one corner as the low point. Leaves give off water into it. Drain it before sunset. Easier than a vegetation bag, but amounts stay small. Treat it if you can.

WARNING: Use only plants you know are not poisonous.
