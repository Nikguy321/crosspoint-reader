# Settle and strain

Mud and silt clog filters and hide germs from bleach and sunlight. Clear the water first, then treat it.

1. Let it stand in a container for several hours, or overnight, until the mud sinks.
2. Pour or dip off the clear top water without stirring up the bottom.
3. Strain it through a clean, tightly woven cloth, a coffee filter, or a bandanna folded several times.
4. Musty or swampy taste? Stir in crushed charcoal from your fire, wait 45 minutes, then strain again. It may help the taste a little.

A seep hole also helps. Dig a hole in sand or gravel about 3 feet (1 m) from a muddy stream or pond. The ground strains the water that seeps in. Keep the hole covered.

WARNING: Settling, straining and charcoal remove dirt, not germs. Always boil or disinfect the water afterwards. A fuel or chemical smell means find other water.

# Improvised filter
![Tripod filter and hanging bag filter](atp-p68-1)

Tie three cloths one above another on a tripod, or use a bag or bottle with a small hole in the bottom. From the top down: grass, then sand, then crushed charcoal. Pour water in the top and catch the drips. Throw away the first cloudy water.

WARNING: A homemade filter does not remove germs. Always boil or disinfect the water afterwards.
