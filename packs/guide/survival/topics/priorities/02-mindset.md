# Fear and stress

Fear is normal. Your body gets ready to act: your heart pounds, you breathe faster, muscles tense and your attention narrows. That surge helps for a short time, but it wears you out.

Watch for signs that stress is taking over:

- trouble making decisions
- forgetting things or making careless mistakes
- constant worry and no energy
- snapping at others or pulling away from them
- an urge to rush off without a plan

To take back control:

1. Stop. Sit down if you can.
2. Take slow, deep breaths, with each breath out longer than the breath in. Repeat several times.
3. Name the fear, out loud or to yourself.
4. Do one small, useful task: put on a layer, drink water, look at the map.
5. Then make a plan.

NOTE: Panic spreads through a group. A calm voice and a clear job for each person help everyone settle.

# Stay positive

Skills matter, but the will to keep going matters just as much. People with little gear have come through because they refused to give up.

- **Be realistic.** See the situation as it is. Hope for the best and prepare for the worst.
- **Set small goals.** "Fire before dark." "Fill the water bottle." Each one done is a win.
- **Keep a routine.** Set times to drink, eat, tend the fire, check signals and rest.
- **Rest and sleep** when you can. Tired people make mistakes.
- **Help others.** Give everyone in the group a job. Looking after someone else lifts your own spirits.
- **Improvise.** Look at ordinary things and think of new uses for them.
- **Think of home.** The people waiting for you are a reason to keep going.

NOTE: Be patient. Waiting in a good shelter is hard, but it is often the right choice. A sudden urge to move on can lead to exhaustion and injury.
