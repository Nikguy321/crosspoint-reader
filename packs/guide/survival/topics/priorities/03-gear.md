# Ten essentials (1)

The National Park Service lists ten things to carry outdoors. Adjust them to the trip and the season.

- **Navigation:** a paper map and a compass, plus a GPS or phone if you have one. The map still works when batteries die. Learn to use them before the trip.
- **Light:** a headlamp keeps your hands free. Carry spare batteries. A light is also a signal at night.
- **Sun protection:** sunglasses, sunscreen and a hat. Long sleeves and long pants help too.
- **First aid kit:** restock used items, replace anything out of date, and add the medicines you need. A first-aid course is worth more than a bigger kit.
- **Knife and repair kit:** a knife or multi-tool, tape, and small parts to fix your gear.

# Ten essentials (2)

- **Fire:** a lighter or waterproof matches, plus fire starter, for warmth and signals. Check fire rules and burn bans before you go.
- **Emergency shelter:** an emergency blanket or bivy sack, a tarp, or a tent. Carry one even on a day hike.
- **Extra food:** at least one extra day of food that needs no cooking. Salty snacks replace salt lost in sweat.
- **Extra water:** water, plus a way to treat more. In heat, drink small amounts often, before you feel thirsty, but not more than 1.5 quarts (1.4 L) an hour.
- **Extra clothes:** a warm layer, hat, gloves and rain shell beyond what you expect. Bright colors are easier to spot.

Also carry a whistle and a charged phone (airplane mode saves battery). Phones often have no signal in the backcountry; consider a satellite messenger or personal locator beacon.

NOTE: Pack the ten essentials on every trip, even short ones. A twisted ankle can turn a short walk into a night outside.

# Pocket survival kit

Keep a small kit on your body, in case you lose your pack. Use a small waterproof container. Choose items that do more than one job.

- whistle and signal mirror
- fire starter (lighter, waterproof matches or ferrocerium rod) and dry tinder in a sealed bag
- emergency blanket
- water treatment tablets
- two large plastic bags, folded flat, for carrying water, a rain cover or a ground sheet
- 20 to 30 feet (6 to 9 m) of strong cord
- small flashlight
- adhesive bandages and gauze
- a day's supply of any medicine you must not miss
- pencil and a waterproof card for notes and messages
- small compass, safety pins, needle and thread

NOTE: Check the kit each season. Replace used items, weak batteries, and tablets or medicines past their date.
+++
# Leave a trip plan

Before you go, give a written trip plan to someone you trust who is not coming with you. Include:

- where you are going and your route
- where you will park, and your vehicle's make, color and plate
- who is with you, with phone numbers
- when you leave and when you expect to be back
- what you carry, such as shelter, a beacon, and the colors of your clothes and tent
- the time to call for help if they have not heard from you

If you are overdue, they should try to reach you first, then call 911 and share the plan.

Stick to the plan. If you change it, tell your contact. When you get back, let them know, so no one starts a search by mistake.

NOTE: Park staff will not hold your trip plan. You may also leave a copy inside your vehicle, out of sight, but never on the dashboard or windshield: it tells thieves you are away.
