# Dress in layers

Your clothes are the shelter you always have with you. Several thin layers trap more warm air than one thick layer, and you can add or remove them as needed.

- **Base layer** next to the skin: wool, silk or synthetic fabric that moves sweat away from you.
- **Insulation:** a fleece, wool sweater or insulated jacket.
- **Shell:** a windproof, waterproof jacket with a hood, and rain pants.

Wear layers loose. Tight clothes and boots slow blood flow and make cold injury more likely.

Cover your head, ears, neck and hands: a warm hat, gloves or mittens, and a scarf or neck gaiter. Wear wool or synthetic socks and waterproof boots. Carry a spare set of dry clothes in a sealed bag.

WARNING: Avoid cotton, including jeans. Cotton soaks up water and does not keep you warm when wet. Hypothermia can happen even above 40 F (4 C) when you are chilled by rain, sweat or cold water.

NOTE: Reference only - not a substitute for first-aid training. Call for help first.

# Stay dry, not sweaty

Wet clothing pulls heat from your body many times faster than dry clothing. Remember **COLDER**:

- **Clean:** dirt and grease crush the air spaces that keep you warm.
- **Overheating:** avoid it. Before you sweat, open zippers, take off a layer or your hat, or remove mittens. Put layers back on when you stop.
- **Loose layers:** easy to adjust, and they don't squeeze blood flow.
- **Dry:** put your shell on before you get soaked. Dry damp items inside your jacket with body heat, or near a fire, never over it. Hang them only where the back of your hand stays comfortable for 3 seconds or more, and watch them. Synthetics melt easily. Dry boots slowly.
- **Examine:** check clothes for wear and tears every day.
- **Repair:** fix small holes early with tape or needle and thread.

NOTE: Keep spare socks dry in a sealed bag. When you stop, change wet socks and dry your feet.
