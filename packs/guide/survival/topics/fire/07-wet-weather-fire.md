# Wet wood

Rain soaks the outside of wood, not the inside.

- **Standing dead wood** sheds rain and stays dry inside. Wood lying on the ground is often wet or rotten and only smolders.
- **Dead lower branches** of conifers, sheltered by the tree above, often stay dry.
- **Pitch wood**, the resin-soaked wood in old pine stumps and knots, burns even when damp.
- **Split wood** to reach the dry core, then shave feather sticks from it.
- Look under logs, rock overhangs and thick trees for dry tinder.
- Keep tinder dry inside your clothes until you need it.
- Prepare twice the usual tinder and kindling. A tepee lay burns damp wood well.
- Stack damp kindling and fuel near the fire to dry, far enough away that it cannot catch.

Wet fuel gives off white smoke. That is fine once the fire is hot.

# Fire on snow
![Platform of green logs for a fire on snow](fm92-p99-2)

Build on a platform so meltwater does not drown the fire. Lay green logs side by side on the snow, then one or two more layers, each across the one below. Do not build under snow-laden branches. The heat can drop snow onto the fire.
+++
# Carry fire with you
![Fire bundle](atp-p133-1)

Carrying embers saves matches. On a large sheet of bark from a dead tree, lay damp grass or leaves, then dry punk (crumbly rotten) wood. Put live coals in the punk, roll it up and tie it. Check it often. It can keep embers for hours.

WARNING: Never carry embers through dry grass or brush. Drown the bundle when done.
