# Tinder

Tinder is fine, dry, fluffy material that catches from a spark or a small flame. Gather at least a cupped handful for each fire, and more in wet weather.

- **Natural:** dry grass, dead ferns, dry moss, birch bark, the shredded inner bark of cedar, fine wood shavings, scrapings of pitch wood (resin-soaked pine), seed fluff from cattail, milkweed or thistle.
- **Carried from home:** cotton balls (better with petroleum jelly worked in), dryer lint, paper, a candle stub, fire starter tabs.

Fluff tinder up so air gets between the fibers. Keep some dry in a sealed bag at all times.

Damp tinder? Carry it inside your clothes, close to your body, to dry it.
+++
# Kindling

Kindling carries the flame from the tinder to the fuel. It must be dead and dry.

- **Sizes:** pencil lead, then pencil, then thumb thick, about 12-18 in (30-45 cm) long. Gather a bundle of each size as thick as a soda can.
- **Snap test:** a dead branch that snaps cleanly is dry. One that bends is green or wet.
- **Under conifers:** the small dead branches low on the trunk, sheltered by the tree, stay dry and give all three sizes.
- **Split it:** split thicker wood to reach the dry inside.
- **Feather sticks:** shave thin curls down a dry stick, leaving them attached at one end. They catch fast.

In winter a dormant branch can look dead. Scrape the bark: green underneath means it is alive and will not burn well.

WARNING: Never burn anything that may be poison ivy, poison oak or poison sumac, including dead vines on firewood. The smoke can cause severe lung reactions.

# Fuel and building up
![Base and brace](atp-p120-1)

Fuel is dead wood from wrist to forearm thick. Gather three times what you think you need before you light anything.

Lay tinder on a dry base, such as bark or flat wood, against a forearm-thick brace. Light it, then lay kindling across the brace so air flows underneath.
