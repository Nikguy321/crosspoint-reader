# Bow drill parts
![Bow drill parts and use](atp-p131-1)

- **Drill:** straight stick 3/4 in (2 cm) thick, 10-12 in (25-30 cm) long.
- **Fireboard:** about 1 in (2.5 cm) thick and 4 in (10 cm) wide.
- **Socket:** stone or hard wood with a dip.
- **Bow:** strong stick about 3 ft (90 cm) long, with cord.

Board: dry, dead, soft, non-resinous wood, such as willow, cedar or cottonwood.
+++
# Using the bow drill

1. Carve a dip about 1/2 in (1.3 cm) in from the board's edge. Cut a V-notch from the edge into the dip's center.
2. Set a fire pan (a chip of wood or bark) under the notch. Have tinder and kindling ready.
3. Kneel; put one foot on the board beside the dip.
4. Twist the cord once around the drill. Set the drill in the dip and the socket on top. Brace the socket hand against your shin.
5. Saw the bow with long, even strokes, using the whole cord. Press down steadily.
6. When smoke appears, go faster until the smoke is thick.
7. Lift the drill away gently. If the dust keeps smoking by itself, you have a coal.
8. Use the fire pan to tip the coal into the tinder nest. Close it loosely and blow with long, gentle breaths until it flames.

NOTE: Soap or grease in the socket cuts friction. Keep it off the drill's lower end.
+++
# Hand drill
![Hand drill](atp-p132-1)

Spin a straight, dry stalk between flat palms, pressing down, in a fireboard like the bow drill's. Your hands slide down; move them back to the top quickly and keep going. Dry stalks such as yucca or elderberry work.

It is hard work and best in dry weather. Use it as a last resort.
