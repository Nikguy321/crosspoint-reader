# Pick the spot

Look for a flat, dry spot out of the wind, close to your shelter and your firewood.

- Stay clear of overhanging branches, snow-laden trees, steep slopes, rotten stumps, logs, dry grass and leaves.
- Keep the fire well away from shelter walls, bedding and gear.
- Scrape leaves, needles, twigs and duff down to bare soil in a circle **10 ft (3 m) across**.
- Avoid deep layers of rotting leaves, peat or a mat of roots. Fire can creep into them and smolder long after the flames are out.
- Level the ground so fuel does not roll off the fire.
- A ring of rocks helps hold the fire. Use only dry, solid rocks.

WARNING: Wet rocks, or rocks that soak up water, can burst in the heat and throw sharp pieces.

WARNING: Never burn a fire, coals or a stove inside a tent, snow cave or closed shelter. Carbon monoxide can kill you in your sleep.

# Reflectors and fire walls
![Straight and L-shaped fire walls](atp-p119-1)

A wall of logs or solid rock on the far side of the fire throws heat back toward you and your shelter, and blocks the wind. Green logs last longest. Build it before the fire gets hot, and leave the fire enough air to burn.

# Dakota fire hole

A pit fire burns hot, smokes little and is easy to control in wind.

1. Pick bare mineral soil well away from trees, so no roots run under the pit.
2. Dig a pit 8-10 in (20-25 cm) wide and 10-12 in (25-30 cm) deep.
3. Upwind, dig an air tunnel 6-8 in (15-20 cm) wide that joins the pit's bottom.
4. Build the fire in the pit.

WARNING: Fire can creep along buried roots and break out later, far from the pit. Never dig a fire pit among tree roots, peat or deep duff.

When you leave, drown and stir it until cold to the touch. Only then fill it in.
