# The fire triangle
![The fire triangle: oxygen, heat and fuel](atp-p118-1)

A fire needs **heat**, **fuel** and **air**. Take any one away and it goes out. Too little air smothers it.

Fire keeps you warm, dries wet clothes, boils water, signals rescuers and lifts your spirits.
+++
# Fire safety and the law

Before any trip, check for fire restrictions and burn bans. They can change daily. A fire during a ban is illegal and can bring a fine. If any fire escapes, you can be billed for the cost of fighting it.

In an emergency a fire can save your life. Even then:

- **Not in dry, windy weather** unless your life depends on it. Blowing sparks start wildfires.
- **Use an existing fire ring** if there is one. **Keep it small.**
- **Never leave it**, not even for a few minutes, and not even when no flames show.
- **Have water and a digging tool ready** before you light it. Stack spare wood away from the fire.
- **Never pour fuel** such as gasoline on a fire, even a smoldering one. Keep fuel bottles and spray cans well away.

WARNING: If a fire escapes, leave at once. Fire runs fastest uphill and downwind; do not flee that way. Head for bare ground, rock or water, then call 911 with your location.
+++
# Put it out cold

1. **Drown** the fire, pouring water slowly. Wet every ember, coal and stick, not just the red ones.
2. **Move the rocks** of the ring. Embers hide underneath.
3. **Stir** the ashes with a stick or tool, add more water, and stir again.
4. **Feel** for heat: hold the back of your hand just above the ashes. When you feel none, touch the ashes and coals with your bare hand. Check for burning roots.
5. **Repeat** until everything is cold to the touch.

No water? Mix in plenty of dirt or sand and keep stirring until everything is cool. Do not just bury the coals. They can smolder underground and flare up later.

NOTE: If it is too hot to touch, it is too hot to leave. For burns, see FIRST AID: Wounds and burns.
