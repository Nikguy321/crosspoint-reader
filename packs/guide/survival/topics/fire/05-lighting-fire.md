# Matches and lighters

Get everything ready first: a dry base, tinder, three sizes of kindling and fuel. Light from the upwind side.

- Carry waterproof or strike-anywhere matches in a waterproof case with a striker.
- Strike the match, then tip the head down so the flame climbs the stick. Shield it with your body and cupped hands.
- Hold the flame to the bottom edge of the tinder. Do not push it into the tinder, which can smother it.
- Save matches: light a candle or a feather stick with one match, then light the fire from that.
- A butane lighter can fail in the cold. Keep it warm in an inside pocket. A flint-wheel lighter with no fuel still makes sparks.
+++
# Ferro rod

A ferro rod (metal match) sparks thousands of times, even in cold. Wipe it dry if it gets wet. Scrape off the dark coating on a new rod first.

1. Rest the rod's tip in the tinder.
2. Press the striker, or a knife's spine, at a steep angle near the rod's top.
3. Hold the striker still and pull the rod back hard.
4. When the tinder smokes, blow gently.

The sparks are very hot. Keep them off your clothes, gear and fuel bottles.
+++
# Lens
![Focusing sunlight with a lens](atp-p128-1)

Works only in bright sun. Use a magnifier, reading glasses (not distance glasses), or a lens from binoculars or a camera.

Move the lens until the light makes the smallest, brightest dot on dark tinder. Hold it still until the tinder smokes, then blow gently.

NOTE: Never look at the sun through a lens.
+++
# Flint and steel
![Striking a hard rock with steel](atp-p129-1)

Strike a hard, sharp-edged rock such as flint, quartz or chert with a glancing blow from carbon steel. Stainless steel sparks poorly. Catch sparks on char cloth.

**Char cloth:** put cotton loosely in a small tin with a nail hole in the lid. Set it on hot coals. When smoke stops, lift it off with a stick and let it cool before opening.
+++
# Steel wool and a battery

Touch fine steel wool across both terminals of a small battery, such as a 9-volt. The wool glows red and can flare up fast. Have tinder ready to catch it, and keep your fingers clear.

WARNING: Never make sparks or flame near a vehicle battery. It can give off hydrogen gas that explodes.

WARNING: Carry batteries apart from steel wool, keys and coins. A short circuit can start a fire in your pack.
+++
# Practice first

- Practice every method at home, in wind, rain and cold, before you need it. Try it with cold, wet hands and with gloves on.
- Carry at least two ways to light a fire, such as waterproof matches and a lighter or ferro rod, plus fire starters that catch quickly.
- Keep one fire starter in a pocket, not only in your pack.
- Keep it simple when you are hurt, cold or tired. Use the easiest method you have.
- Do not waste matches on a fire you have not fully prepared.
- Friction methods, such as the bow drill, take skill, strength and very dry wood. Learn them as a backup, not a plan.
