# Debris hut frame
![Debris hut: frame of ridgepole and ribs, then covered](atp-p149-1)

1. Prop a ridgepole a little longer than you are tall on a stump, rock or forked sticks, about waist high.
2. Lean sticks along both sides as ribs, steep enough to shed rain, wide enough for your body.
3. Lay brush across the ribs.
+++
# Debris hut cover

The warmth comes from the pile of dead, dry material: leaves, pine needles, grass, ferns.

1. Heap it over the frame **at least 3 ft (1 m) thick**. Thicker is warmer.
2. Lay branches on top so wind cannot blow the pile away.
3. Stuff the inside with 1-2 ft (30-60 cm) of the same material to lie on and burrow into.
4. Crawl in feet first. Pull a pile of leaves into the doorway behind you, or stuff a bag or spare jacket with leaves as a door plug.

Gather everything before you start. It takes a lot more material than you expect, often a couple of hours of work.

NOTE: If you can see light through the walls from inside, add more.

WARNING: A debris hut is a pile of tinder. Keep any fire well away from it, and never take a flame inside.
+++
# A-frame
![A-frame shelter in four steps](atp-p143-1)

1. Lash two poles together at eye level to form an upside-down V.
2. Rest a long ridgepole in the V, the thick end on the ground behind.
3. Lean poles along both sides.
4. Cover with a tarp, or with boughs or bark (next page). In snow country, heap snow over the boughs and make a door plug.
+++
# Shingle the roof

Natural cover keeps rain out only if it is laid like shingles on a house.

1. Start at the **bottom** of the frame.
2. Lay boughs, bark slabs or grass bundles in a row, tips pointing down.
3. Lay the next row higher, overlapping the row below so water runs off the top of each piece onto the one beneath.
4. Keep going to the ridge.

A steep roof, about 40 to 60 degrees, sheds water best. A flat one leaks.

Add cover until you cannot see light through the roof from inside. Add more before heavy rain or snow, and check that the frame can take the extra weight.

Strip sharp stubs from the poles so they don't tear your tarp or you.
