# Tree-pit shelter
![Tree-pit shelter dug around an evergreen trunk](atp-p145-1)

Snow is shallow under a big evergreen.

1. Dig out around the trunk, down to the ground if you can.
2. Pack the snow walls firm.
3. Line the floor with boughs.
4. Lay more boughs over the top as a roof.

WARNING: A person who falls head first into a deep tree well can be trapped and suffocate. Dig in from the side.

# Snow cave
![Snow cave: entrance block, cold sump, platforms, vent](atp-p150-1)

Dig into a drift about 10 ft (3 m) deep, not on or below an avalanche slope.

- Make the sleeping bench higher than the entrance, so cold air sinks out.
- Arch the roof. Keep walls and roof at least 1 ft (30 cm) thick.
- Block the door with a snow block or pack.
+++
# Air and safety

A sealed snow shelter can run out of fresh air, and snow drifting overnight can close the door.

- **Vent:** poke a hole about 2 in (5 cm) wide through the roof with a stick or pole. Leave the stick in it and clear the hole often, especially after snowfall.
- **Keep a digging tool inside** with you, in case the entrance drifts shut or part of the roof drops.
- **Mark the outside** with a pole or bright cloth, so rescuers can find you and no one walks on the roof.
- **Too warm?** If you cannot see your breath, it is warm enough to melt the roof. Cool it down.
- **Dig slowly** with your layers open, so sweat and snow don't soak you.

WARNING: Never burn a stove, candle, lantern, heater or fire inside a snow shelter or tent. A vent or open door is not enough. Carbon monoxide has no smell and can kill you in your sleep. Cook outside.
