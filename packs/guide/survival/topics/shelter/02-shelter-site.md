# A good site

Look for a spot that is:

- **Flat and dry,** big enough to lie down with your gear.
- **Out of the wind,** behind a rise, rocks or thick trees.
- **Near materials:** poles, boughs, leaves, firewood.
- **Near water, but not on the bank.** Stay well above the high-water line.
- **Easy to spot from the air,** with an open area nearby for signals.
- **Slightly raised** rather than in a hollow, which collects cold air and rainwater.

Clear rocks, sticks and cones from where you will lie. Check for ant nests, wasp nests, poison ivy or poison oak, and animal trails or dens.
+++
# Dangers above and below

Look up, then look uphill.

- **Dead trees and dead limbs** can fall in wind or under snow and kill you. Move away from them.
- **Rockfall:** avoid the base of cliffs and slopes with loose rock below them.
- **Avalanche paths:** a clear strip, or one of young brush, running down a snowy slope, and the flat ground at its foot. Stay off and below snow slopes steeper than about 30 degrees, and away from cornices.
- **Flash floods:** never camp in a dry stream bed, wash or gully, or close to a stream when rain is coming. Water can rise fast from a storm miles away.
- **Valley bottoms** are the coldest place on a clear night.
- **Ridges, peaks and lone tall trees** are the likeliest places for lightning to strike. Camp below them.

WARNING: If flooding threatens, move to higher ground at once. Never walk into fast water: 6 in (15 cm) of moving water can knock an adult over.

# Natural shelters
![Natural shelters: brush, rock overhang, fallen tree, tree well](atp-p138-1)

Use what nature built: a rock overhang, a fallen tree, a root hollow, thick conifers with low limbs. Check for animals and loose rock first. Pile brush or rocks on the windward side to block the wind.

WARNING: In a thunderstorm, never shelter under a rock overhang, in a cave mouth or under a lone tree. No place outside is safe: get off high ground.
