# Insulate from the ground

Cold ground and snow pull heat out of you fastest. Never sleep directly on them.

- **Pile it high:** boughs, dry leaves, grass or ferns at least **12 in (30 cm)** thick. It squashes flat under you.
- **Bough bed:** start at the foot end. Push the cut ends of evergreen boughs into the ground at a slant, close together, tips up and toward the head end. Make it a little longer and wider than you.
- **Frozen ground:** lay dead branches first and stick the boughs into those.
- **Use gear too:** a sleeping pad, empty pack, rope coil, spare clothes or a seat cushion all help.
- **Fluff it** every day and add fresh material.

Avoid poison ivy and poison oak, and shake out insects first.

NOTE: National parks ban cutting live trees and boughs, national forests need a permit, and parks may ban gathering even dead wood. Use your tarp, pad and gear first; cut green wood only in a real emergency.

# Warm and safe

- Eat something and drink warm, sweet drinks before bed. No alcohol or caffeine: they make you lose heat faster.
- Wear a dry hat and dry socks. Put on every spare layer.
- Fill a bottle made for hot drinks with hot, not boiling, water. Close it tight and wrap it in a sock: never against bare skin.
- Huddle together under shared covers if there are two or more of you.
- Keep fires outside, about 3 ft (1 m) from the opening, and the opening side-on to the wind.

WARNING: Never run a stove, grill, heater, lantern or engine inside a tent, shelter or garage. An open flap is not enough. Carbon monoxide can kill you in your sleep. Headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea or confusion: get to fresh air and call 911.

NOTE: Stranded in a car in snow? Stay with it. Run the engine and heater only about 10 minutes an hour, with the tailpipe clear of snow and one window open slightly.
